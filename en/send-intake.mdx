---
title: "Send a form to an athlete"
description: "Send an intake form or a feedback right now, pick a date, or schedule the repetition."
---

Besides the automatic rules, you can send a form to **one specific athlete** — when
they're back from an injury, change goals, or you want to arrange follow-up just with
them.

## How to send it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the athlete">
    In **Athletes**, open the person and go to the **Assessments** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the sub-tab">
    **Intake** or **Feedback**, depending on what you're about to send. The sub-tab is what
    sets the type — the button and the list follow it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Send intake form (or Send feedback)">
    That opens the send window, already showing the forms of that type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose which one">
    Select the form from your library.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say when">
    **Now**, **On a date** or **Repeat**. Confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The three ways to send

### Now

It goes out right away. The athlete gets the notification and answers in the app.

### On a date

You pick the day and forget about it. The send goes out **in the morning** of that day.

<Note>
  The date starts **tomorrow**, not today. Since a scheduled send goes out in the morning,
  picking today in the afternoon would only deliver tomorrow — you'd ask for one thing and
  get another. For today, use **Now**, which really is immediate.
</Note>

### Repeat

You choose the day of the first send and how often it repeats — in **days**, **weeks** or
**months**.

<Tip>
  This is how you build tailored follow-up: a monthly feedback for that one athlete,
  without creating a rule that applies to your whole roster.
</Tip>

## How long the repetition lasts

**There's no end date.** It repeats for as long as the person is your athlete, and stops
on its own when the relationship ends.

We prefer that to an expiry date precisely so nothing stops silently: a deadline would run
out a year from now, the form would simply stop arriving, and you'd find out when someone
happened to mention it.

<Note>
  **A suspended athlete doesn't receive forms** — neither immediate nor scheduled. Someone
  who is suspended can't see their workouts, and asking "how was your workout?" in that
  situation is out of place. The repetition is paused and, when you reactivate them, they
  get **one** form — the current one, not the pile from the time it was stopped.
</Note>

## Seeing and canceling what's scheduled

On the same sub-tab, the **Upcoming sends** block shows what is still going out and on
which dates.

The first send of each schedule also shows **Edit**. It belongs to the whole schedule, not
to that one date — which is why it doesn't repeat on every line.

- **The repetition** you can change whenever you want: the next send already goes out at
  the new pace.
- **The date of the first send** can only change while it hasn't happened yet. After that
  the field appears disabled, because the send that already went out is what drives the
  calendar from then on.

When you click **Cancel**, you choose the scope:

- **Cancel just this one** — that date doesn't happen. The following ones go ahead as
  usual, on the right dates.
- **Cancel the repetition for [Name]** — that athlete stops receiving it. If the
  repetition came from a rule in your library, **the other athletes keep receiving it**.

<Tip>
  Canceling one send in the middle doesn't mess up the calendar: the next one lands on the
  date that was already scheduled, not a cycle later.
</Tip>

## Seeing the answers

The sub-tab holds every send for that athlete, newest first:

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Waiting for an answer in the app** | Sent, not answered yet. |
| **Answered** | Click to open the answers, photos included. |
| **Send canceled** | You undid it before they answered. |
| **Not answered** | It expired when the next one was asked. |
| **Not sent** | The intake form of the month took priority. See [Intake form or feedback?](/en/form-types) |

<Tip>
  Sending the same form again creates a **new round**. The old answers stay stored, so you
  can compare before and after.
</Tip>

## How the athlete is reminded

Worth knowing, because it's the question that comes up when an athlete takes a while to
answer.

<Steps>
  <Step title="One prompt a day, at most">
    The form opens by itself in the app **once a day**. Showing up on every launch would
    turn into nagging: someone who opens the app four times to train would get the same
    prompt four times — and that doesn't make anyone answer faster.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They can answer later">
    The prompt has an **Answer later**. Trapping someone who opened the app to train only
    makes them close the app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="But it doesn't disappear">
    Until they answer, a **fixed marker stays on their home screen**. Tapping it reopens the
    form right away, even if the prompt already showed up that day.
  </Step>
</Steps>

They also get a **notification** when the form arrives, and tapping it opens the form
directly.

<Note>
  When the intake form is holding the workouts back, the app spells that out — in the
  prompt and on the marker. That way the athlete understands why they aren't seeing a
  workout, instead of thinking the app is broken.
</Note>

## Manual sending doesn't lock the workout

A form sent by hand is a request, not a requirement: the athlete keeps normal access to
their workouts.

If you want the workout to unlock only after the answer, that's done with a **rule**, in
**Forms → Intake forms → When to require**. See
[when to require the intake](/en/recurring-intake).
