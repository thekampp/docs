---
title: "How the intake form works"
description: "Build your own questionnaires, choose when they're required, and deliver the right workout based on the answers."
---

The Kampp intake form isn't a fixed questionnaire. You build **your** questions, decide
**who** has to fill it in and **when**, and you can have the workout delivered based on
what the athlete answered.

Everything lives in **Settings → Intake**, and the screen has two parts:

- **My intake forms** — your library. Each form is a questionnaire with its own name
  (e.g. "Initial intake", "Biweekly check-in").
- **When to require** — the rules. This is where you say who gets the form (new athletes,
  everyone, or people who bought through an integration) and whether it repeats.

## The full path

<Steps>
  <Step title="You build the form">
    Create the questions however you like: text, choices, scale, slider, photos and more.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You say when it's required">
    A rule links the form to a trigger — a new athlete, all your athletes, or a purchase
    through an integration (Kiwify, Hotmart, Sellyo).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The athlete answers in the app">
    They get a prompt and answer right in the app, one question per screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The right workout is delivered">
    If you set up **delivery by answer**, each answer unlocks the matching workout folder —
    without you lifting a finger.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changes for the athlete

When the intake form comes from a rule (required), the workout you delivered stays
**locked until they answer**. That way nobody starts training before you know their
history, injuries and goal.

As soon as they submit, the workout is released the next morning at 6am (in their own
timezone) — and the answers land in your dashboard.

<Tip>
  A form sent manually (from the athlete's profile) **doesn't lock** anything. The lock
  only happens when the form comes from a rule.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Intake forms include health questions (injuries, conditions, medications). That data is
  sensitive — treat it with the same care you'd give a medical record.
</Note>
